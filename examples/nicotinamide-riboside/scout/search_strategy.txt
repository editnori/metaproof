1. Decisions the protocol needs before the search is final
----------------------------------------------------------

1.1 What counts as "NR"
  Include NR and NR chloride under any name: nicotinamide riboside, nicotinamide
  riboside chloride, nicotinamide beta-riboside, nicotinamide ribonucleoside,
  ribosylnicotinamide, NR, NRCl, Niagen, Tru Niagen / TruNiagen.
  CAS numbers (checked in PubChem 2026-11-02): NR 1341-23-7; NR chloride 23111-00-4.
  MeSH has no descriptor for NR. It is a supplementary concept,
  "nicotinamide-beta-riboside" (C018613). The PubMed tag [nm] searches it.
  The tag "nicotinamide riboside"[nm] finds nothing; use "nicotinamide-beta-riboside"[nm].

  "NR" alone is not searchable (nuclear receptor, not reported, and so on). Use it only
  next to an NR-specific term.

1.2 Combination products. Search for them; decide in the protocol how to analyse them.
  - NRPT ("Basis", Elysium Health): NR plus pterostilbene. Also sold as EH301 for ALS.
  - CMA ("combined metabolic activators"): L-serine, N-acetylcysteine, NR, L-carnitine.
  - NR plus ketone salts (BREAK-AD), NR plus resveratrol (one arm of NICE).
  In these trials the control arm gets neither component, so the contrast is
  "mixture vs placebo" and the NR effect cannot be separated (Cochrane Handbook
  chapter 3, on defining interventions and comparators and on which comparisons
  will be made). Suggested rule: primary analysis = NR
  alone vs placebo or no NR. Include a co-intervention only when both compared arms
  get it (for example NR+exercise vs placebo+exercise). Report mixtures as a separate
  comparison or a sensitivity analysis.
  Searching matters here: EH301 (PMID 30668199) never says "nicotinamide riboside" in
  its PubMed record, so it is found only by the product-name line.

1.3 NMN and other NAD+ precursors: keep them as a separate comparison, not in the NR pool
  Suggestion: do not search for NMN, nicotinamide, niacin, NRH or NAD+ infusions as
  eligible interventions. Search for them only if the protocol adds a separate,
  clearly labelled "other NAD+ precursors" comparison. Reasons:
  a) They are different molecules, with different doses and different routes to
     NAD+. In the one head-to-head human trial found (PMID 41540253, NR 1000 mg vs
     NMN 1000 mg vs nicotinamide 500 mg for 14 days), NR and NMN raised whole-blood
     NAD+ by similar amounts and nicotinamide did not. Pooling them would assume a
     class effect that the question ("does NR work?") does not need and the data do
     not support.
  b) Niacin causes flushing and changes lipids at the doses used, which would bias
     the lipid and blinding results of an "NAD+ precursor" pool.
  c) Earlier meta-analyses already pooled NR with NMN and other precursors (examples
     found in this search: PMID 35303905, glucose and lipids; PMID 37593976, blood
     pressure, CRP and carotid IMT; PMID 40275690, muscle mass and function). An
     NR-only review answers a different, narrower question. Use their reference
     lists as a source of NR trials (section 7).
  d) The Cochrane Handbook asks that the comparisons be defined in the protocol and
     that interventions be grouped for synthesis only on stated grounds (chapter 3).
  If NMN is added later, treat multi-arm trials with NR and NMN arms (PMID 41540253;
  PMID 41858901 is non-randomized) with the kit's multi-arm settings
  (multi_arm: split) so the shared placebo is not counted twice.

1.4 Design filter. Include parallel, crossover, factorial and cluster randomized
  trials. Non-randomized, single-arm and open-label pre/post studies are listed in
  nr_candidate_trials.csv (status "nonrandomized") so the PRISMA flow can count
  them, but they are not eligible for the RCT synthesis. Randomized trials with no
  placebo or no-treatment arm (e.g. NR doses compared with each other, R057) carry
  status "no_eligible_comparator" and are excluded for that reason.

1.5 No date or language limits. No limit on population (healthy or disease).

Notes on PubMed
  - "tru niagen"[tiab] and "EH-301"[tiab] are not in the PubMed phrase index (no
    hits), so they are left out. truniagen and EH301 cover them.
  - Source of #2: Lefebvre C, Glanville J, Briscoe S, et al. Technical Supplement to
    Chapter 4: Searching for and selecting studies. In: Cochrane Handbook for
    Systematic Reviews of Interventions, current version, section 3.6.1 (Cochrane
    Highly Sensitive Search Strategies for identifying randomized trials in
    MEDLINE). Check the box number and wording in the current supplement before
    the final run.
  - #3 was added on purpose. The twin study (PMID 36638183, non-randomized) and
    several small open-label studies were missed by #2 alone. No randomized NR trial
    was missed by #2: the 549 non-animal NR records outside #2 were checked by a
    keyword screen of titles and abstracts, and the flagged records were read by hand.
  - Screening done here: all 193 records of an earlier, narrower run were screened by
    title and abstract. The 160 records added by the final lines were screened by the
    keyword screen above, with flagged records read in full. Two people still need
    to screen all 353 independently, in duplicate (Handbook chapter 4).
  - Recall check against the registry: every PubMed record that a ClinicalTrials.gov
    NR record lists as a trial report is in #5, except two. PMID 34694070 (CMA in
    NAFLD) never names NR in its PubMed record. PMID 40473693 reports muscle biology
    from an NRPT trial, not an NR comparison. If CMA trials are eligible, add this
    line to #1 (115 non-animal records on 2026-11-02, including PMID 34694070):
      "combined metabolic activator"[tiab] OR "combined metabolic activators"[tiab]
      OR "metabolic cofactors"[tiab]
    ("metabolic cofactor supplementation" is not in the PubMed phrase index.)

Notes on Embase
  - Lines 8-9 follow the pattern of the Cochrane Embase RCT filter, extended with
    pilot and open-label terms. Take the exact current filter wording from the
    Technical Supplement to Chapter 4 (section on search filters) and replace lines
    8-9 with it if it differs.
  - The NR literature is small. Expect line 7 NOT 11 to return a few thousand
    records at most. If it does, screen all of it and skip the filter (lines 8-10).
    The filter is a fallback if the yield is too big.
  - Keep conference abstracts (do not add "not conference abstract.pt."). For a
    supplement this small they often flag trials that were never published in full
    (Handbook chapter 4 on grey literature; chapter 13 on missing results).
  - Field tags: tn = drug trade name, rn = CAS registry number, kw = author keywords.

5. ClinicalTrials.gov (API v2, as run 2026-11-02 UTC)
-----------------------------------------------------

Query A (intervention field):
  query.intr = nicotinamide riboside OR Niagen OR "NR chloride" OR EH301 OR NRPT OR "Tru Niagen"
  -> 128 studies, 17 with results posted
Query B (all fields):
  query.term = "nicotinamide riboside"
  -> adds 5 studies; one is relevant (NCT07802548, sirtuin activators including NR
     plus diet, randomized, completed, 45 enrolled)
Union: 133 records (ctgov_nr_registrations.csv). 17 have results posted
(ctgov_results_posted.csv).
Script: .venv/bin/python examples/nicotinamide-riboside/scout/query_ctgov.py

Web form equivalent: "Intervention/treatment" box with the query A string; then a
second search with "nicotinamide riboside" in "Other terms".

What the registry search adds
  - 102 of the 133 registrations are randomized. Only 24 of those 102 have a stated
    link to a PubMed record in nr_candidate_trials.csv (NCT ID in the abstract or the
    PubMed DataBank field); 10 more have only a probable link, matched by the screener
    on title, design, n or population, which is not evidence that the trial was
    published (column link_status in ctgov_nr_registrations.csv; registration_link in
    nr_candidate_trials.csv). Several completed randomized trials have no publication
    linked: NIRVANA (NCT04818216, results posted), N-DOSE (NCT05589766), N-DOSE AD
    (NCT05617508), Slow Age (NCT05593939), the long-COVID factorial trial
    (NCT05703074, 310 enrolled), the ketone salts + NR chloride crossover in long-chain
    fatty acid oxidation disorders (NCT05411835, 5 enrolled) and others. They are rows
    R071-R092 of nr_candidate_trials.csv. Use posted results where they exist and
    contact the investigators. This matters for the risk of bias due to missing
    results (Handbook chapter 13).
  - Some PubMed articles do not state their registration, and the link is only
    probable. These are marked UNVERIFIED in nr_candidate_trials.csv, and their
    ctgov_check says the registry record was fetched but the trial identity is not
    verified. Confirm each one from the full text or with the authors before it is
    used to count a registered trial as published.

Also tested (2026-11-02) and judged not worth adding as standing queries:
  query.intr = pterostilbene (7 extra, none with NR); "NAD+ precursor" (8 extra: NMN,
  niacin and MIB-626 trials, plus NCT03310034 "NAD-precursors", terminated,
  randomized, n = 14, precursor not named; worth one look); "combined metabolic
  activator" (CMA trials NCT07062198 and NCT07889284 do not name NR in the record).
  If CMA trials are eligible, also search "combined metabolic activator*" and
  "metabolic cofactor*".

8. Reporting
------------
Report the search with PRISMA-S: Rethlefsen ML, Kirtley S, Waffenschmidt S, et al.
PRISMA-S: an extension to the PRISMA Statement for Reporting Literature Searches in
Systematic Reviews. Syst Rev 2021;10:39.
Before the final run, have an information specialist peer review the strategy
(PRESS: McGowan J, Sampson M, Salzwedel DM, Cogo E, Foerster V, Lefebvre C. PRESS
Peer Review of Electronic Search Strategies: 2015 Guideline Statement. J Clin
Epidemiol 2016;75:40-46).
Count reports and studies separately. Several trials here have two to four reports
(trial_key in nr_candidate_trials.csv). The study, not the report, is the unit of
analysis (Handbook chapter 5, section 5.2.1).

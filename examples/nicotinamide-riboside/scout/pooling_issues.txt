NR review: pooling problems by outcome family, and the kit setting for each
==========================================================================

Built from the screened abstracts and registry records in nr_candidate_trials.csv
(record IDs R001-R092). Full texts were not read. Every "trial reports X" below
means "the PubMed abstract or the registry record says X". Confirm it in the full
text at extraction.

Kit names used below:
  extraction columns: see templates/extraction_data_dictionary.csv
  config keys: see metaproof/config.py (defaults block, per-outcome overrides)
  example: examples/nicotinamide-riboside/SYNTHETIC_nr_like_extraction.csv + examples/nicotinamide-riboside/SYNTHETIC_nr_like_config.yaml
           (invented numbers; shapes only)


A. Problems that cut across every family
----------------------------------------

A1 Crossover trials
   Trials: Martens 2018 (R023), Remie 2020 (R016), Nascimento 2021 (R017),
   Elhassan 2019 (R020), Ahmadi 2023 (R013, three periods: NR, CoQ10, placebo),
   Wu 2025 SCD/MCI (R008), Shoji 2025 Werner (R007, 26-week periods),
   Dolopikou 2020 (R022, single dose), NIH immunity trial NCT02812238 (R025).
   Problem: a paired analysis needs the within-person correlation, which is rarely
   reported. Analysing the periods as if they were parallel groups wastes the
   pairing and gives too much variance, but it is conservative (Elbourne DR, Altman DG,
   Higgins JPT, et al. Int J Epidemiol 2002;31(1):140-149; Cochrane Handbook
   chapter 23, section 23.2). Carry-over and period effects need a judgement in the
   RoB 2 crossover tool. Washout lengths were not in the abstracts.
   Kit:
   - design = crossover; one row per treatment condition (not per sequence); same n on both rows.
   - Use paired_md + paired_se on the NR row when the trial reports a paired
     difference with its SE or CI. This needs no correlation.
   - Otherwise enter the period means and SDs. corr_within (row) wins over the
     config corr_within (default 0.5). When a crossover uses the assumed r, both
     engines rerun at r = 0.2 and 0.8 by default (corr_within_sensitivity: auto); a
     list such as [0.2, 0.8] always runs, and [] turns it off with a log warning.
   - For ROM and SMD the kit derives r from paired_se when both period SDs are given.
     Crossover SMD carries Hedges' J on N - 1 df, like parallel g (crossover_smd_j).
   - Built from period means, the paired MD assumes no period effect (balanced
     sequences); the kit says so in method_notes. Prefer a period-adjusted paired
     estimate (Cochrane Handbook v6.5, section 23.2.5).
   - A row with only within-period change (mean_change, sd_change) cannot be used;
     the checker rejects it. Enter period means and SDs or the paired estimate.
   - Record washout_weeks, period_length_weeks, n_per_sequence and crossover_analysis
     on study_characteristics; RoB 2 domain S needs them. R007 (Shoji 2025) switched
     after 26 weeks and its abstract states no washout.
   - First-period-only data: decide in the protocol before extraction (Handbook v6.5,
     section 23.2.4), never after a carry-over test, and assess those results with the
     parallel RoB 2 tool. Enter them as design = parallel, with a note.
   - Three-period trial (R013): extract the NR and placebo periods only. The CoQ10
     period is not an NR comparison.
   - Combining parallel and crossover trials in one analysis follows Curtin F,
     Altman DG, Elbourne D. Stat Med 2002;21(15):2131-2144. Use subgroups: [design]
     to show both.

A2 Change from baseline vs final values
   Trials report either. Examples: NOPARK (R001) MMRM change; Lin 2025 (R006)
   change +/- SD; Remie (R016) period values with change +/- SE; Orr (R012) change.
   Problem: MD can mix change and final values across trials. SMD should not
   (Cochrane Handbook chapter 10, section 10.5.2). Imputing an SD of change needs a
   baseline-to-follow-up correlation (Handbook chapter 6, section 6.5.2.8).
   Kit:
   - data_basis: auto. For MD: reported change (with sd_change or se_change), else
     final values, else change with an imputed SD; nothing is imputed when the study
     offers an assumption-free option. For SMD: final values only (Handbook v6.5,
     section 10.5.2), so auto is safe for SMD.
   - data_basis: change forces change scores. Under SMD it needs a reported SD of
     change in every arm: an imputed SD would be the standardiser, so the assumed r
     would move g itself (Handbook v6.5, section 23.2.7.2); such studies are excluded
     with a reason.
   - corr_prepost (default 0.5) and corr_prepost_sensitivity (default [0.2, 0.8]).
     A row-level corr_prepost wins; compute it from a trial that reports baseline,
     final and change SDs (metaproof.conversions.corr_from_sds).
   - The SD imputation happens only when baseline_mean, baseline_sd, mean and sd are all filled.
   - Change reported with its SE: put it in se_change (extension column); the
     engines convert it.
   - The log warns "SMD mixes change and final values" only when parallel rows mix
     the two; crossover rows (data_basis = paired) no longer trigger it.

A3 Multiple NR doses and multi-arm trials
   Trials: Conze 2019 (R021, NR 100/300/1000 + placebo), Dellinger 2017 (R029, NRPT
   1X/2X + placebo), NRPT NAFLD (R030, 1x/2x + placebo), Simic 2020 (R032, four dose
   steps sharing four placebo patients), Christen 2026 (R003, NR, NMN, nicotinamide,
   placebo), NICE (R010, NR+placebo, NR+resveratrol, placebo), Lin 2025 NEET (R006,
   NR+exercise, placebo+exercise, NR alone), Lin 2026 Friedreich (R027, 2 x 2
   factorial).
   Problem: a shared placebo arm must not be counted twice (Handbook chapter 23,
   section 23.3.4). Some arms are not NR contrasts at all.
   Kit:
   - multi_arm: combine (default) merges the NR arms (Handbook Table 6.5.a), so the
     control is counted once; this is the main analysis (protocol H). multi_arm:
     split divides the shared control n across the NR arms and is for prespecified
     dose analyses only: the split rows still share placebo participants and are
     correlated (Handbook v6.5, sections 23.3.4 and 23.3.5). The kit reports
     k_studies beside k and applies every minimum-k rule to studies; leave-one-out
     drops a whole trial. combine_arms: {study_id: {new_label: [arm, arm]}} merges
     named arms only.
   - A combined arm has no single dose: dose_mg_day is left blank (dose_min and
     dose_max are written) and the comparison drops out of dose models, with the
     reason in log.txt. The control row's dose is never used.
   - Several reported between-group MDs (paired_md, paired_se) against one control
     are refused (Handbook v6.5, section 23.3.4); keep one prespecified comparison or
     extract arm-level data.
   - The kit compares every is_control = 0 row with the control row. So do NOT
     extract arms that are not an NR contrast: the NMN and nicotinamide arms of R003,
     the NR+resveratrol arm of R010 (unless running a mixtures sensitivity analysis),
     the NR-alone arm of R006 (its only comparator gets exercise too). Record them in
     notes. The SYNTHETIC example row S07 shows this.
   - Factorial (R027): the four groups are distinct people. Enter two study_ids
     (e.g. Lin2026_noEx: NR vs placebo; Lin2026_Ex: NR+exercise vs placebo+exercise),
     or pick one per the protocol. Do not use both and also a pooled factorial estimate.
   - Dose: moderators: [dose_mg_day] with multi_arm: split runs a meta-regression,
     only with at least 10 studies (metareg_min_k; Handbook v6.5, section
     10.11.5.1). Below that the kit writes a 'not run' row. A model for dependent
     effects (multilevel or robust variance estimation; Hedges LV, Tipton E, Johnson
     MC. Res Synth Methods 2010;1(1):39-65) would handle split arms properly; the kit
     does not implement it.
   - Salt form: most trials dose NR chloride. NR cation 255.25 g/mol, NR chloride
     290.70 g/mol (PubChem, checked 2026-11-02), so 1000 mg NR chloride = about 878 mg
     NR. Put the dose as reported in dose_mg_day and the salt form in notes. Convert
     only if trials mix forms.

A4 Multiple timepoints
   Trials: Wu 2025 long-COVID (R005: 5, 10, 20 weeks; only 0-10 weeks is a
   randomized placebo comparison; delayed start after that), Conze 2019 (R021:
   effects "within 2 weeks", maintained to 8 weeks), NOPARK (R001: MMRM to 52
   weeks), Norheim (R009: 6 weeks plus 12-week follow-up off treatment), Jensen
   (R031: 2 h to 30 days after injury).
   Problem: one timepoint per study per analysis (Handbook chapter 6, section 6.2 on
   unit of analysis). The choice must be set in the protocol, not after seeing the data.
   Kit:
   - One row per arm per timepoint. first/last order timepoints in weeks: the label
     is read with its unit ('day 21' is 3 weeks, earlier than 'wk6'), or the
     timepoint_weeks extension column is used when filled (the checker fills it from
     duration_weeks). A study the rule cannot place (e.g. a label 'end' with no
     timepoint_weeks) goes to excluded.csv with the reason; it no longer stops the run.
   - timepoint: last | first | <exact label> | [list of labels]. A list is a priority
     order: each study keeps the first listed label it reports, so one timepoint per
     study (Handbook v6.5, section 6.2.4). Two rows with the same arm label in one
     analysis are refused. Run a second
     analysis key with outcome: <same outcome> and a different timepoint rule for a
     sensitivity or a "short-term vs long-term" split (SYNTHETIC example:
     SYNTHETIC_whole_blood_nad_earliest).
   - For R005 extract only wk10 for the randomized comparison.
   - Follow-up after stopping NR (R009 week 18) is a different question (persistence);
     give it its own outcome key.

A5 Tiny n and few trials
   Most NR RCTs randomize 8-60 people. Exceptions: NOPARK 410, NRPT NAFLD 111,
   Dellinger 2017 120, NICE 90. Per outcome, expect k well under 10.
   Problems: tau^2 is poorly estimated; Wald intervals are too narrow; funnel-plot
   tests have no power; one large trial (NOPARK) can dominate.
   Kit:
   - estimator: REML (default) with hksj: true and hksj_rule: revman (defaults): HKSJ
     only when tau2 > 0 and k >= 3, otherwise the Wald interval (Handbook v6.5,
     section 10.10.4.4; Hartung J, Knapp G. Stat Med 2001;20(24):3875-3889). With
     tau2 = 0 untruncated HKSJ can be narrower than the fixed-effect interval, and at
     k = 2 it uses t(1) = 12.71 (Jackson D, Law M, Rucker G, Schwarzer G. Stat Med
     2017;36(25):3923-3934). pooled.csv writes the Wald, HKSJ and truncated-HKSJ
     intervals side by side, so "report both when HKSJ is narrower" needs one run.
   - min_k_pool: 3 (default): with 2 studies the forest plot shows both without a
     diamond and pooled.csv is blank with a note.
   - pooled.csv gives every estimator (REML, DL, PM, ML, FE) as a built-in sensitivity.
   - prediction_interval: true. Computed from k >= 3 but flagged "not interpretable"
     below 5 studies (Handbook v6.5, section 10.10.4.3); the tau2 CI is flagged the
     same way.
   - egger_min_k: 10 keeps the small-study test off below 10 studies (counted as
     studies, not split comparisons); lowering it needs egger_allow_small_k: true
     (Sterne JAC, Sutton AJ, Ioannidis JPA, et al. BMJ 2011;343:d4002).
   - SMD uses Hedges g with the exact J correction (kit default), which matters at n < 20.
   - leave1out.csv and influence.csv show whether one trial drives the result.

A6 Ratio and percent outcomes
   Trials often report NAD+ as fold change or percent change (Conze R021: +22%,
   +51%, +142%; Dellinger R029: about +40% and +90%; Wu R005: 2.6-3.1 fold; NR-SAFE
   R011: up to 5-fold), and inflammatory markers on a log scale (Norheim R009:
   sputum IL-8 -52.6%, 95% CI -75.7% to -7.6%).
   Problems: percent change alone cannot be pooled without the arm means and SDs. A
   model-based percent difference is a log ratio, not a mean difference.
   Kit:
   - measure: ROM (log ratio of means; Hedges LV, Gurevitch J, Curtis PS. Ecology
     1999;80(4):1150-1156; Friedrich JO, Adhikari NKJ, Beyene J. BMC Med Res Methodol
     2008;8:32). Needs arm means > 0 and uses final values only (data_basis: final).
   - Geary's check: the kit flags any arm with mean x sqrt(n) / SD < 3 (rom_geary:
     flag) or drops the comparison (rom_geary: exclude). NAAD and MeNAM in placebo
     arms, near the limit of detection, are the usual failures; use MD for those
     analytes. The Lajeunesse bias correction (Lajeunesse MJ. Ecology
     2015;96(8):2056-2063) runs automatically as a sensitivity analysis.
   - Fold change or percent change entered as 'mean' gives a ratio of mean fold
     changes, a different estimand; the checker rejects rows that look like that.
   - A reported parallel between-group MD with its SE (e.g. MMRM- or ANCOVA-
     adjusted) can now be entered in paired_md and paired_se on one NR row, and is
     used as it stands for MD analyses (one such comparison per study). There is
     still no column for a ready-made log ratio for ROM.
     Example: the Norheim IL-8 percent difference converts to
     log ratio = ln(1 - 0.526) = -0.747. This is a model-based, probably log-scale and
     baseline-adjusted ratio of geometric means, not an arithmetic-mean ROM (Higgins
     JPT, White IR, Anzures-Cabrera J. Stat Med 2008;27(29):6072-6092), so it belongs
     in a separate analysis or a sensitivity analysis only. Back-calculate its SE from
     the CI with t on the trial's df, not 1.96, because the trial is small (Handbook
     v6.5, section 6.5.2.3): SE = (ln(0.924) - ln(0.243)) / (2 x t(0.975, df)). With
     1.96 the value would be 0.341, which understates the SE.

A7 Skewed outcomes reported as median and IQR
   Likely in inflammatory markers, insulin, HOMA-IR, triglycerides, AEs counts, and
   some NAD+ data. Registry results for Thomas 2026 (R004, skin nerve fibres) and
   Lin 2026 Friedreich (R027, peak VO2 change) are medians.
   Kit:
   - median with q1/q3 and/or min/max; median_method: luo (default) or wan.
     Wan X, Wang W, Liu J, Tong T. BMC Med Res Methodol 2014;14:135. Luo D, Wan X,
     Liu J, Tong T. Stat Methods Med Res 2018;27(6):1785-1805.
   - Both assume near-normal data, and medians are reported because data are skewed
     (Handbook v6.5, section 6.5.2.5). Median + range without IQR is excluded unless
     allow_range_only: true (Handbook v6.5, section 6.5.2.6); the kit adds it back as a
     sensitivity analysis. study_effects.csv has converted_from_median and
     median_asymmetry columns, and the K5 sensitivity analyses (Wan for both, exclude
     converted rows) run automatically. median_asymmetry is a pointer, not the Shi
     2023 skewness test (Shi J et al. Stat Methods Med Res 2023;32(7):1338-1360),
     which has to be applied by hand.
   - Kit gap: median columns are for final values. A median (IQR) of change, as in
     the R027 registry results, has no column. Use the publication's means if given,
     or ask the authors.
   - If a trial reports geometric means or results on the log scale, see Higgins JPT,
     White IR, Anzures-Cabrera J. Stat Med 2008;27(29):6072-6092 before converting.

A8 Units
   Kit: no automatic unit conversion. Convert at extraction with
   templates/unit_conversions.csv and write the factor in notes. Different matrices
   (whole blood, PBMC, plasma, muscle, CSF, urine) are different outcomes, not units.

A9 Several reports of one trial
   NCT02303483 (Dollerup: R019, R043, R044), NCT03579693 (Ahmadi: R013, R041,
   R042), NADPARK (R015, R045), Martens 2018 (R023, R046), NCT02835664 (Remie R016,
   BAT R017), NCT02812238 (R025, R026), NCT03489200 (R033 and R034 give different
   designs and n for one NCT), NCT04044131 (one NCT for the AD and PD trials).
   Kit: one study_id per trial (same value in RoB 2 and GRADE files); take each
   outcome from the report that has it; never enter the same participants twice
   (Handbook chapter 5, section 5.2.1).

A10 Co-interventions and mixtures
   NRPT (R029-R032, R033, R034, R061), CMA (R035-R039), NR+ketones (R040),
   NR+resveratrol arm (R010), exercise trials (R006, R027, R028).
   Kit: no special setting. Decide eligibility in the protocol (search_strategy.txt
   section 1.2). For a mixtures sensitivity analysis, give the mixture trials their
   own population or a text moderator and use subgroups, or run a separate outcome
   key on a separate extraction file.


B. By outcome family
--------------------

B1 Blood NAD+ metabolome (NAD)
   Trials (R-numbers): R003, R005, R009, R011, R012, R014, R015, R021, R022, R023,
   R029, R032, R002, R004 (plasma: no rise), R020 (muscle), R019/R044 (muscle: no
   rise), R018 (muscle), R046 (neuronal EVs), R015 (brain MRS, CSF).
   Problems: different matrices (whole blood, PBMC, plasma, erythrocyte NADH,
   muscle, brain, CSF, urine catabolites); different assays (LC-MS, enzymatic
   cycling; registry results for NCT04818216 report ug/mL); results given as fold or
   percent change (A6); several doses (A3); several timepoints (A4); crossovers (A1);
   very large between-trial heterogeneity expected.
   Kit: one outcome key per matrix (whole_blood_nad, pbmc_nad, muscle_nad, ...);
   measure: ROM, data_basis: final; moderators: [dose_mg_day]; subgroups: [design]
   or [population]; prediction_interval: true. Do not use ROM for NAAD or other
   analytes near zero in the control arm (A6). The NAD+ ug/mL to umol/L factor is in
   unit_conversions.csv.

B2 Blood pressure and arterial stiffness (BP)
   Trials: R023 (SBP, PWV; exploratory), R002 (secondary), R006 (daytime SBP
   primary; PWV; nighttime BP), R016 (ambulatory BP, crossover), R007 (CAVI), R062
   (non-randomized). Upcoming: Freeberg protocol R051 (NCT03821623, 94-118 planned).
   Problems: office vs 24-h vs daytime vs nighttime BP are different outcomes;
   change vs final mix (A2); crossover (A1); co-intervention arm structure in R006
   (A3); PWV (m/s) and CAVI (unitless) are not the same construct.
   Kit: outcome keys sbp_office, sbp_daytime, sbp_24h, ...; measure: MD (mmHg),
   data_basis: auto; corr_prepost from a trial that reports all three SDs, with
   sensitivity; arterial stiffness as its own key with SMD (data_basis: auto, which
   means final values for SMD) if PWV and CAVI are pooled, or MD per index.

B3 Glucose and insulin sensitivity (GLU)
   Trials: R019 (clamp; 12 weeks; 2000 mg/day), R043 (OGTT, beta-cell function,
   incretins: same trial as R019), R016 (clamp; crossover), R028 (glucose biomarkers;
   exercise in both arms), R055 (non-randomized twins).
   Problems: different measures of insulin sensitivity (clamp M-value or Rd,
   HOMA-IR, Matsuda, OGTT AUC); clamp units (mg/kg/min vs umol/kg/min; per kg body
   weight vs per kg fat-free mass); glucose mg/dL vs mmol/L; insulin uIU/mL vs pmol/L
   (factor 6.00 or 6.945); HOMA-IR is skewed (A7); one trial spread over two reports
   (A9).
   Kit: fasting_glucose and fasting_insulin as MD after unit conversion
   (unit_conversions.csv); insulin_sensitivity as SMD on final values (data_basis:
   auto or final) if clamp and index measures are pooled, or MD per method; median
   inputs for HOMA-IR (median_method), with the skew caveats in A7.

B4 Lipids (LIP)
   Trials: R021 (LDL-C; "did not elevate"), R013 (lipidomics: triglycerides,
   ceramides; crossover), R030 (ceramide 14:0; NRPT).
   Problems: few trials with standard lipid panels; pterostilbene may itself raise
   LDL-C (letter, PMID 30482564, title "Pterostilbene raises low density lipoprotein
   cholesterol in people"), so NRPT trials confound any lipid result; lipidomics
   yields hundreds of species (pick the protocol-named ones); mg/dL vs mmol/L;
   triglycerides skewed.
   Kit: ldl_c, hdl_c, total_c, triglycerides as MD in mmol/L after conversion;
   triglycerides via median columns if needed; keep NRPT trials out of the primary
   lipid analysis (A10). The kit does not adjust P values unless bh: true (protocol
   L says no adjustment); if used, the family is primary analyses only, one per
   outcome. Neither fixes the real risk here, selective choice among lipid species,
   assays and timepoints within a trial: pick one result per study with the protocol
   hierarchy (Part 2, section L), record it in study_characteristics
   result_selection, and carry any deviation to RoB 2 question 5.2.

B5 Inflammatory markers (INF)
   Trials: R009 (sputum IL-8 primary, plasma IL-6), R020 (circulating cytokines),
   R015 (serum and CSF cytokines), R041 (IL-2, IL-13, CRP; crossover estimated
   differences with 95% CI), R016 (plasma inflammatory markers), R014 (NLRP3
   expression), R024 and R025 (ex vivo Th17 / IL-17 secretion after stimulation),
   R026 (ex vivo monocyte IFN-beta), R065 (PBMC cytokine expression; non-randomized).
   Problems: many markers per trial (selective reporting risk); circulating levels,
   gene expression and stimulated ex vivo secretion are different constructs; skewed
   data reported as medians or on a log scale (A6, A7); values below the detection
   limit; pg/mL vs ng/mL; hs-CRP mg/L vs mg/dL (x 10); sputum vs plasma.
   Kit: one outcome key per marker and matrix (crp_serum, il6_plasma,
   il8_sputum, ...); measure: ROM (final values) or MD on converted units; the R041
   crossover estimates go in paired_md / paired_se on the NR row; ex vivo assays as
   separate keys, probably narrative only.

B6 Body composition (BODY)
   Trials: R016 (fat-free mass %; crossover), R019 (DXA, MRI), R028 (intramuscular
   fat, muscle cross-sectional area), R033 (muscle/fat weight ratio; EH301), R034
   (anthropometry; NRPT + diet), R055 (non-randomized).
   Problems: kg vs % of body mass; DXA vs MRI vs BIA vs skinfolds; between-person
   variance is large and baseline-to-follow-up correlation is very high, so final
   values are a poor basis and the default r = 0.5 badly overstates the SD of change.
   Kit: data_basis: change; set corr_prepost for this key from a trial that reports
   all three SDs (expect a high value) and widen corr_prepost_sensitivity around it;
   fat_mass_kg and lean_mass_kg as MD; percent measures as their own keys.

B7 Muscle and physical function (MUSC)
   Trials: R013 (VO2 peak mL/min/kg; crossover), R027 (peak VO2 L/min; factorial;
   registry medians), R022 (VO2max, torque, fatigue index; single dose), R010
   (6-minute walk; three arms), R012 (walking speed), R008 (step counts), R031 (strength
   after injury; NRPT), R033 (MRC strength; EH301), R019/R044, R016, R020, R018
   (muscle mitochondrial respiration).
   Problems: VO2 in L/min vs mL/kg/min (needs body mass to convert); factorial and
   three-arm structures (A3); medians of change (A7); practice effects in walking
   tests (R012 placebo improved); one-sided 90% CI in NICE (R010); mitochondrial
   respiration units differ by protocol.
   Kit: one key per test (vo2peak_rel, vo2peak_abs, six_mwd, grip_strength, ...);
   MD within a test, SMD across tests (final values); factorial as two
   study_ids; NICE: extract NR+placebo vs placebo only for NR-alone; convert the
   one-sided interval to an SE before entry (the 90% one-sided bound is not a 95%
   two-sided interval; check whether the paper also reports a two-sided CI).

B8 Cognition (COG)
   Trials: R001 (MDS-UPDRS part I includes non-motor items; other), R002 (cognitive
   composite primary), R005 (ECog, RBANS, TMT-B; delayed start), R008 (RBANS;
   crossover), R012 (MoCA), R035 (ADAS-Cog; CMA), R036 (MoCA; CMA), R040 (processing
   speed; NR + ketones), R062 (non-randomized).
   Problems: different instruments and scales; direction differs (higher is worse
   for TMT-B time and ADAS-Cog); learning across periods in crossovers (period
   effects); delayed-start design (R005, only wk10 randomized); most trials are
   8-12 weeks, too short for clinical decline.
   Kit: measure: SMD with data_basis: auto (final values for SMD); data_basis: change
   only where every trial reports an SD of change; timepoint per protocol (A4).
   Kit gap: no "higher is better" column. Multiply means (or change means) by -1 at
   extraction for instruments where higher is worse, and say so in notes. Do not do
   this for ROM.

B9 Other outcomes (OTHER)
   - Neurological rating scales: MDS-UPDRS (R001, R011, R015; R036 UPDRS for CMA),
     ALSFRS-R (R033), ataxia scales (non-randomized R058, R059).
     MD within a scale; NOPARK's MMRM-adjusted difference can go in paired_md and
     paired_se on one parallel NR row (MD only; A6).
   - Adverse events and tolerability (nearly every trial; primary in R011, R014, R021).
     Kit gap: the engines handle continuous outcomes only (MD, SMD, ROM). Dichotomous
     outcomes (participants with any AE, serious AE, withdrawals due to AE) need
     risk ratios or risk differences (Handbook chapter 10 on dichotomous data and
     rare events), which the kit does not compute. Plan a separate tool or a
     narrative table.
   - Liver fat and enzymes (R030, R038, R019), renal function (R013 eGFR, R007
     creatinine, R032), homocysteine and one-carbon metabolism (R011, R021, R045),
     cerebral blood flow (R002, R012), brain imaging (R015, R040), epigenetic age
     (R009, R012, R069), skin ulcers (R007), lactation (protocol R053).
     Mostly single trials: report narratively unless k >= 2 for one construct.


C. What to settle in the protocol before extraction
---------------------------------------------------
  1. NR alone vs mixtures (A10) and whether NMN is a separate comparison
     (search_strategy.txt section 1.3).
  2. The outcome key list, one key per construct and matrix (A8, B1-B9).
  3. The timepoint rule per outcome (A4).
  4. The source of each assumed correlation, and the sensitivity range (A1, A2).
  5. Which multi-arm and factorial arms are extracted (A3).
  6. How adjusted or log-scale effects are handled: adjusted MDs via paired_md and
     paired_se (MD only); ratios of geometric means as a separate or sensitivity
     analysis (A6).
  8. Whether first-period crossover data are ever used (A1), and the hierarchy for
